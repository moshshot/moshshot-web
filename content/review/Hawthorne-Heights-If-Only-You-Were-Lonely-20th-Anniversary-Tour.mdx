It's hard to believe it's been 20 years since Hawthorne Heights released If Only You Were Lonely. The album helped define the mid-2000s emo scene, and two decades later, the band is back on the road celebrating its anniversary. On October 22, they made a stop at House of Blues Boston, bringing Creeper and letlive. along for the night.

First up were UK gothic rockers Creeper, my FAVORITE band in the entire world. And after watching their performance, I think the entire crowd got a pretty good idea of why. Creeper are one of those bands that you really need to experience live to fully appreciate. Between their gothic aesthetic, ridiculously catchy songs, and theatrical performances, there's genuinely nothing quite like them. One of my favorite parts of the night was seeing fellow Creeper fans scattered throughout the crowd, decked out in face paint and fake blood. There's something so fun about seeing people fully embrace the band's horror-inspired world, especially at a show where they weren't even the headliner. It's a reminder of just how passionate and dedicated their fanbase is.

Frontman Will Gould is an absolute powerhouse, with a voice that somehow sounds even better live than it does on record. His stage presence is incredible, and he has a way of making even the biggest venues feel intimate. Keyboardist and vocalist Hannah Greenwood is equally impressive, and the two of them together are something special. Their harmonies are beautiful, and Hannah's voice adds so much to the band's sound. The set included "Mistress of Death," "Headstones," "The Ballad of Spook & Mercy," and "Cry to Heaven," each showing off a different side of what makes Creeper so good. They manage to blend punk, gothic rock, and theatrical storytelling into something that feels completely their own. My only complaint? Their set wasn't nearly long enough. Creeper deserve to be playing much bigger stages, and if their Boston performance was anyone's first introduction to the band, I have a feeling they walked away with a new favorite.

Next were letlive., and their set was pretty much the complete opposite. Frontman Jason Aalon Butler is known for his unpredictable live performances, and Boston got to experience that firsthand. He spent much of the set running around, throwing himself across the stage, and getting up close with the crowd. The band tore through songs like "Renegade 86'," "Banshee (Ghost Fame)," and "Muther," bringing a much heavier sound to the evening. In between all the chaos, Butler also took a few moments to speak to the audience about connection and acceptance. Their set was loud, messy in the best way, and a lot of fun to watch.

Finally, Hawthorne Heights took the stage to celebrate the album everyone had come to hear. They kicked things off with "This Is Who We Are" and worked their way through If Only You Were Lonely in its entirety. Hearing songs like "We Are So Last Year," "Pens and Needles," and "Saying Sorry" live brought back a lot of memories for longtime fans, many of whom were singing along from start to finish.

Frontman JT Woodruff sounded great throughout the set and took some time between songs to share stories from the band's early days. At one point, he mentioned that he'd actually missed his honeymoon to record the album, which gives you an idea of how much went into making it. It was nice hearing some of the history behind a record that's meant so much to fans over the years.

The band continued through the album, eventually reaching "Decembers," which brought the anniversary portion of the set to a close. After spending most of the night singing along, the crowd seemed happy to just take in the moment.

But obviously, a Hawthorne Heights show wouldn't be complete without a few more fan favorites. The band returned for an encore featuring "Niki FM," "Like A Cardinal," and, of course, "Ohio Is for Lovers." Unsurprisingly, that last one got one of the biggest reactions of the night, with the crowd singing practically every word.

Overall, it was a great night for fans of alternative music, with three bands that each brought something different to the stage. Creeper delivered the gothic theatrics, letlive. brought the intensity, and Hawthorne Heights gave everyone a chance to revisit an album that still holds up 20 years later.

For anyone who grew up listening to If Only You Were Lonely, this tour is a pretty good reminder of why those songs meant so much in the first place.
